## War Footing Strategic Defense Begins Before the First Shot

Most strategy games hand you an army and ask what you'll do with it. War Footing hands you a budget, a calendar, and a border that is about to become very busy. Understanding war footing strategic defense means accepting an uncomfortable truth: the battle is decided in procurement ledgers, training rotations, and political favors long before anyone fires a shot.

In this turn-based strategy game from developer Strategist's Den Games, you are the Minister of Defense — not a field commander. You never order a tank to flank a position. You decide whether the country can afford the tank at all, whether the crew is trained, and whether the political coalition will survive the purchase. That tension is the heart of war footing strategic defense, and it's what makes the game's mechanics worth studying rather than just playing.

That distinction matters because it flips the usual skill set. Reflexes and unit micro won't save you. Budget discipline, sequencing, and foresight will.

## The Core Loop: One Week at a Time

Each turn represents a single week, and weeks are the game's scarcest resource. The invasion date is fixed; your preparation window is not. Every week you spend on one priority is a week you did not spend on another, which means war footing strategic defense is a scheduling problem as much as a spending one.

| Turn phase | What you decide | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Budget review | How to split limited funds across procurement, training, and civil stability | Sets the ceiling for everything downstream |
| Procurement | Buy from foreign suppliers or domestic industry | Determines equipment quality and availability |
| Training & assignment | Raise and equip units from company to brigade level | Converts money into actual combat power |
| Politics | Manage diplomatic obligations and domestic stability | Keeps the budget flowing |
| Events & intelligence | React to scripted and random developments | Can reshape your timeline overnight |
| Deployment | Position built units by sector | Decides where your strength actually counts |

Notice the ordering. Procurement is not the first decision you make, and it is rarely the most important one. It's a second-order consequence of a budget you set and a political situation you either managed or ignored. Players who treat the procurement screen as the whole game tend to build impressive forces that arrive two weeks too late.

## Procurement: Foreign Suppliers vs. Domestic Industry

Equipment in War Footing comes from two places, and they behave nothing alike. Foreign suppliers offer capability now; domestic industry offers depth later. Choosing between them is the single most repeated decision in any war footing strategic defense playthrough.

| Source | Advantages | Drawbacks | Best used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Foreign suppliers | Fast access to modern kit; fills capability gaps immediately | Expensive, sometimes politically conditional, availability fluctuates | Plugging urgent gaps late in the timeline |
| Domestic industry | Cheaper per unit over time; insulated from diplomatic pressure | Slower to ramp; early output is weaker | Building the backbone of your force early |

The general principle: domestic production rewards early commitment, while foreign purchases reward flexibility. Spend the first half of a scenario buying abroad and you often end up with a capable but shallow force — sharp at the top, brittle underneath. Invest domestically early and your opening weeks look underwhelming, but your depth holds up when the operational phase grinds on. In war footing strategic defense, timing is a resource, and procurement is where you spend it.

## Force Structure: 180+ Equipment Types and the Combined Arms Problem

The game offers more than 180 equipment types spanning small arms, armor, artillery, aircraft, and air defense systems. Each carries distinct trade-offs in cost, capability, and availability, which means there is no universally best purchase — only better and worse fits for the enemy you expect to meet.

| Category | Primary role | Typical trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Small arms & infantry equipment | Holds ground; cheapest way to field numbers | Low cost, limited effect against armor |
| Armor | Breakthrough and mobile defense | Expensive; needs maintenance and trained crews |
| Artillery | Attrition and suppression | Devastating but vulnerable without protection |
| Aircraft | Reach and strike flexibility | High cost and heavy dependency on basing |
| Air defense | Denies the enemy an air advantage | Purely defensive; competes for the same budget |

Community and design discussion around the game keeps returning to one lesson: combined arms beats specialization. A brigade of nothing but armor looks excellent on a spreadsheet and folds when it meets prepared defenses without supporting infantry or artillery. Build formations that can answer more than one question.

## Doctrine, Readiness, and the Numbers Behind the Fight

Three attributes determine what a unit is actually worth: readiness, doctrine fit, and equipment quality. You can buy excellent equipment and still lose because the unit was assigned late, trained for the wrong kind of fight, or placed in a sector where its strengths don't apply.

| Attribute | What it reflects | How you improve it |
|---|---|---|
| Readiness | How prepared a unit is to fight right now | Give formations time after raising; don't deploy raw |
| Doctrine fit | Whether the unit's design matches the fight | Match unit types to terrain and expected enemy |
| Equipment quality | Raw capability of the kit | Balance domestic and foreign procurement |
| Deployment | Where the unit stands when war begins | Read the sector map, not just the unit list |

Treat these as four dials rather than one score. A high-readiness, well-placed unit with mid-tier equipment routinely outperforms a cutting-edge formation that was rushed into position.

## Politics and Stability: The Constraint Nobody Plans For

Military spending does not happen in a vacuum. Diplomatic obligations and political stability compete for the same resources, and ignoring them has consequences — usually financial ones, arriving at the worst possible moment. This is the layer of war footing strategic defense that new players skip and experienced players budget for first.

A practical approach: treat political stability as a resource with a floor, not a luxury. Spending that undermines stability early tends to cost more later than the equipment was worth. When a scenario offers a choice between a cheaper unstable path and a slower stable one, the stable path usually finishes stronger, because you keep the ability to buy things at all.

| Political factor | Typical effect | How to manage it |
|---|---|---|
| Domestic stability | Gates your budget and available options | Avoid repeated short-term gambles |
| Diplomatic obligations | Constrains where and what you can buy | Plan procurement around commitments |
| Public tolerance | Influences how much spending is sustainable | Spread controversial purchases out |

## How Combat Actually Resolves

Battles resolve at the operational level — sector by sector, week by week. You aren't clicking individual units into cover; you're watching the consequences of your build play out across a map. The combat phase of war footing strategic defense is a report card, not a minigame.

| Factor | Influence on outcome | Pre-war lever |
|---|---|---|
| Unit readiness | Overall combat effectiveness | Training time allocation |
| Doctrine fit | Efficiency in specific matchups | Unit composition choices |
| Equipment quality | Raw capability per formation | Procurement decisions |
| Deployment | Which sectors are actually defended | Assignment and positioning |
| Combined arms | Resilience across varied situations | Balanced force structure |

Because resolution is simulation-driven rather than scripted, the same scenario can end very differently depending on choices you made weeks earlier. If a sector collapses, the cause is usually visible in your procurement log.

## Scenarios: Eight Different Problems

The game ships with eight scenarios, each with distinct starting conditions, resource constraints, and enemy configurations. Treat each one as a puzzle with a different correct answer, because each tests a different part of war footing strategic defense.

| Scenario pressure | What changes | What it teaches |
|---|---|---|
| Tight budget | Fewer procurement options per week | Prioritization over completeness |
| Aggressive neighbor | Shorter effective preparation window | Speed versus quality |
| Political fragility | Stability loss costs more | Sequencing your spending |
| Difficult terrain | Sector value shifts | Deployment planning |

## Practical Tips for a Successful War Footing Strategic Defense

- **Decide your doctrine in the first three weeks.** Everything after that is execution, not strategy.
- **Never deploy an untrained unit.** A raw formation is a budget line, not a defense.
- **Invest domestically early, buy foreign late.** Use imports to fill gaps, not to lay foundations.
- **Protect stability above your comfort level.** The budget you lose hurts more than the equipment you skipped.
- **Build combined arms, not showpieces.** Infantry, armor, artillery, and air defense in the same sector.
- **Read the sector map before you assign.** The best unit in the wrong place is a wasted unit.
- **Keep one reserve week.** Events rarely arrive on schedule, and flexibility is worth more than one more purchase.

## FAQ

**Is war footing strategic defense about fighting battles or managing a nation?**
Managing a nation. Combat resolves at the operational level, sector by sector and week by week, while your real decisions happen earlier in procurement, training, politics, and deployment.

**How long does a full scenario take?**
Each turn is one week, and scenarios run through a fixed preparation window followed by the invasion. Total length depends on which of the eight scenarios you pick and how quickly the operational phase resolves.

**Can I win with cheap equipment?**
Sometimes. Readiness, doctrine fit, and deployment often matter more than raw capability. A well-trained, well-placed force with modest kit can outperform a modern force that was thrown together late.

**Should I play the demo before buying?**
It helps. A demo is available, and running a single scenario gives you a feel for the weekly loop — and shows you whether the budget-first rhythm of war footing strategic defense appeals to you before you commit.
