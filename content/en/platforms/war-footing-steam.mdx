Most wargames hand you a battlefield. War Footing Steam hands you a budget, a procurement calendar, and a country that may not survive the year. That is the whole pitch of this turn-based strategy game, and it is why the War Footing Steam release on October 15, 2026 has quietly become one of the more interesting niche wargames of the year.

If you have ever looked at a historical order of battle and wondered whether the army a real ministry could actually afford would hold the line, this is the game built around that question. You never take direct control of a tank or a squad. You decide what exists, who trains on it, and where it stands when the first shot is fired.

Below is a full breakdown of the core loop, the equipment system, the price and specs, and the practical habits that separate a prepared nation from a collapsed one.

## What Is War Footing on Steam?

War Footing is a turn-based strategy game developed and published by Strategist's Den Games. It arrived on Steam on October 15, 2026, and it takes a deliberately unusual angle on the genre: you are not a field commander, you are the Minister of Defense.

Your responsibilities are the budget, the procurement contracts, the political relationships, and the overall force structure of a nation under threat. The War Footing Steam store page frames the stakes plainly — with limited time and resources and tensions rising, can you build a military capable of preserving your country's independence?

The tone leans on late-Cold-War alternate history. The two belligerents are fictional, which frees the design from the baggage of replaying a real conflict, and the violence is described in abstract terms rather than depicted in detail. Expect a lot of numbers and very little spectacle.

There are eight scenarios, each with its own starting conditions, resource constraints, and enemy configuration. That variety carries most of the replay value, because a scenario that opens with a modernized armored corps plays nothing like one that begins with a scraped-together reserve force.

## The Weekly Turn Loop: Budget, Procurement, and Politics

Every turn represents one week. That single design choice shapes everything else: you have a countable number of decisions between now and the invasion, and each one spends time as well as money.

The loop alternates between domestic management and military preparation. You source equipment from foreign suppliers or from your own domestic industry, train and assign units, and manage political stability so the budget keeps flowing. Ignore the political side for too long and the money dries up at the worst possible moment.

| Phase | What you decide | What it affects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Budget & politics | Funding levels, stability, political capital | Whether next week's money arrives at all |
| Procurement | Foreign imports vs. domestic production | Capability now vs. reliability later |
| Training & assignment | Which units form, and how ready they are | Opening combat performance |
| Diplomacy | Which obligations you honor | Supplier access and political goodwill |
| Resolution | End the week | Rising tension and the invasion clock |

Military spending does not happen in a vacuum here. Diplomatic obligations and internal stability compete for exactly the same resources, so every strong army is also a political argument you have to win. Players who treat the politics screen as optional usually discover otherwise around week ten.

Resource management sits at the center of that tension. You are constantly choosing between buying one more armored battalion and keeping a coalition partner happy enough to keep selling you spare parts.

## Equipment, Units, and How Battles Resolve

The equipment catalog is the deepest part of the game. There are more than 180 equipment types spanning small arms, armor, artillery, aircraft, and air defense systems, and each one carries distinct trade-offs in cost, capability, and availability. The best tank on the list is rarely the right purchase if you cannot sustain it.

| Category | Primary role | Trade-off to weigh |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small arms | Equipping infantry formations | Cheap, but caps your ceiling |
| Armor | Breakthrough and holding ground | Expensive and support-hungry |
| Artillery | Shaping sectors before contact | Logistics-heavy |
| Aircraft | Reach and striking power | Costly, and exposed without cover |
| Air defense | Protecting units and infrastructure | Reactive, situational value |

Units are raised at company through brigade level, so you think in formations rather than individual vehicles. Battles resolve at the operational level, sector by sector and week by week. Outcomes depend on the readiness you built, how well your doctrine fits your equipment, the quality of what you bought, and where you chose to deploy it.

That means combined arms is not a flavor choice — it is the difference between a sector holding and a sector folding. Knowing your enemy's configuration before you commit is the other half of the equation, which is why reconnaissance spending tends to pay for itself.

One transparency note worth flagging: the developers state that AI was used for coding assistance only, with artwork produced by a paid human artist. That is a clearer position than many studios offer.

## War Footing Steam FAQ

**Is War Footing on Steam worth buying?**
It depends on what you want from a wargame. If you enjoy planning, budgets, and long-term consequences more than tactical micro-management, the premise is unusually well matched to your taste. Community reports are still limited, so check recent player experience and try the free demo before you decide.

**Does the War Footing Steam version include multiplayer?**
No. It is built as a single-player experience from the ground up, which is consistent with its text-based, decision-heavy design. There is no co-op or competitive mode listed on the store page.

**What are the system requirements?**
The minimum spec is Windows 10 (64-bit), a dual-core 2.0 GHz processor, 4 GB of RAM, integrated graphics, and 500 MB of free storage. If your machine can browse the web comfortably, it can almost certainly run this.

**Is there a demo available?**
Yes. A free demo is listed alongside the full release, and it is the most reliable way to test whether the weekly turn loop holds your attention. Given the game's minimalist presentation, hands-on time tells you far more than screenshots do.
