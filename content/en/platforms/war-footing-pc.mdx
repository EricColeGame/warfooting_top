## What Is War Footing on PC?

War Footing is a turn-based strategy game developed and published by Strategist's Den Games. Instead of commanding troops in the field, you sit at the highest level of military command as the Minister of Defense, responsible for the budget, procurement contracts, political relationships, and overall force structure. The question the game keeps asking is blunt: when the invasion finally comes, is the army you built actually ready?

The setup is intentionally tense. Time and resources are limited, the political temperature rises week by week, and there are eight scenarios, each with its own starting conditions, resource constraints, and enemy configuration. That means the hand you're dealt changes substantially from one run to the next, and so does the correct opening move.

## How the Weekly Turn Loop Works

Every turn in War Footing represents one week. You spend those weeks sourcing equipment, training and assigning units, and managing political stability so the money keeps flowing. When the invasion begins, the army you built is the army you fight with — there's no last-minute scramble to fix a bad plan.

| Phase | What you're deciding | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Budget and politics | How much to spend, and how much stability you can afford to risk | Political stability keeps the budget flowing; ignore it and the pipeline dries up |
| Procurement | Foreign suppliers versus domestic industry | Foreign buys can be faster but carry diplomatic strings; domestic builds cost time |
| Training and assignment | Which units get formed, equipped, and readied | Readiness is a combat multiplier you can't buy on the eve of war |
| Deployment | Where each formation sits, sector by sector | Battles resolve operationally, so position shapes outcomes |
| Resolution | The week's results | Feedback on whether your doctrine and equipment fit the fight you're actually in |

The political layer is what separates this from a pure spreadsheet exercise. Military spending doesn't happen in a vacuum — diplomatic obligations and domestic stability compete for the same resources, and the store page is explicit that ignoring them has consequences. A player who maxes out procurement while letting the political situation rot will often find the budget throttled just when the tanks were supposed to arrive.

## Equipment, Units, and Combat Resolution

Force building runs from company level up to brigade level, and the equipment catalog is genuinely broad: more than 180 equipment types spanning small arms, armor, artillery, aircraft, and air defense systems. Each entry carries distinct trade-offs in cost, capability, and availability, so there's rarely a single obviously correct purchase.

| Category | What it covers | What to weigh |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small arms | Infantry kit and basic equipment | Cost and availability versus marginal capability |
| Armor | Tanks and heavy ground platforms | High cost, high capability, long procurement timelines |
| Artillery | Indirect fire support | Capability that only pays off with correct doctrine fit |
| Aircraft | Air power | Expensive and often availability-limited |
| Air defense | Protection against air threats | Defensive value that's easy to skip and painful to lack |

Combat resolves at the operational level — sector by sector, week by week — rather than through tactical micromanagement. Outcomes depend on the army you assembled: unit readiness, doctrine fit, equipment quality, and where you chose to deploy. That structure rewards combined arms thinking and, critically, knowing your enemy before the first shot.

## Tips for Your First Campaign

A few habits separate a competent defense minister from one who watches their line collapse in week twelve.

- **Buy for the war you expect, not the war you want.** Doctrine fit and equipment quality both feed into combat outcomes, so a mismatched force is expensive dead weight.
- **Protect political stability early.** It's the tap that fills the budget, and it's much harder to restore than to maintain.
- **Balance foreign suppliers against domestic industry.** Each path has a different cost in money, time, and diplomatic obligation.
- **Don't skip air defense.** It's the category players most often defer, and it's the one that hurts most when neglected.
- **Read readiness before you deploy.** A well-placed, well-trained brigade beats a larger, unready one.
- **Use the demo as a tutorial.** The eight scenarios vary enough that early practice on the simplest setup pays off later.
- **Play at your own pace.** The game supports saving anytime, so there's no reason to rush a turn you'll regret.
