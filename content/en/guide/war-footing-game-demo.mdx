## What Is War Footing? The 30-Second Pitch

You are the Minister of Defense. You control the budget, the procurement contracts, the political relationships, and the force structure of a nation staring down an invasion. Each turn represents one week of preparation: sourcing equipment from foreign suppliers or domestic industry, training and assigning units, and managing political stability so the money keeps flowing. When the invasion finally begins, the army you built is the army you fight with — there is no last-minute shopping trip.

The full release ships with eight scenarios, each with distinct starting conditions, resource constraints, and enemy configurations. Units range from company to brigade level, and the equipment catalog runs past 180 types covering small arms, armor, artillery, aircraft, and air defense. The setting is an alternate-history Cold War, with store tags pointing to the 1980s and 1990s. Combat resolves at the operational level, sector by sector, week by week.

## Core Systems You Need to Learn in Your First Hour

Three systems decide whether your first run ends in a stalemate or a rout. Learn them in this order and the war footing game demo stops feeling opaque.

### Procurement Is a Lead-Time Game

Equipment does not teleport. Foreign suppliers and domestic industry offer different costs, capabilities, and availability windows. Ordering armor in the final weeks before tension breaks means it arrives after the shooting starts. Treat every order as a promise about the future, not a purchase for today.

### Politics Is a Resource, Not Flavor Text

Military spending does not happen in a vacuum. Political stability and diplomatic obligations draw on the same pool of capital as your procurement budget. Ignore them and the budget itself becomes a casualty — and a cut budget mid-build can strand half-finished formations.

### Doctrine and Combined Arms Decide Battles

Battles resolve sector by sector and week by week, and outcomes hinge on unit readiness, doctrine fit, equipment quality, and deployment choices. A pile of tanks without air defense, artillery, or logistics is a pile of targets.

| Small arms & infantry kit | Cheap volume, fast to field | Struggles against armor |

| Artillery | Standoff fire support | Vulnerable without screening units |
| Aircraft | Reach, reconnaissance, strike options | Highest cost, needs air defense cover |
| Air defense | Protects everything else you own | Wins no ground on its own |

## A Practical Turn Plan for Your First Demo Run

New Ministers of Defense tend to over-buy and under-plan. Use this weekly priority order to avoid the classic first-run collapse.

| Turn phase | What to do | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Budget review | Confirm funding and political stability before spending | A stability crisis can slash your budget mid-build |
| 2. Procurement | Place orders early; check lead times | Late orders arrive after the invasion starts |
| 3. Training & assignment | Assign units to formations you will actually deploy | Readiness beats raw headcount |
| 4. Politics & diplomacy | Spend remaining capital on stability or obligations | Both compete for the same pool of resources |
| 5. Deployment | Position units by sector before tension breaks | You fight with what is already in place |

Beyond the checklist, a few habits separate a decent run from a disaster:

- **Buy for the fight you expect, not the fight you want.** If the scenario's enemy is armor-heavy, air defense and anti-armor matter more than prestige purchases.
- **Keep a reserve of budget and equipment.** Surprises arrive on schedule in this genre.
- **Protect readiness above all.** An understrength brigade with modern kit often loses to a ready one with older kit.
- **Replay the same scenario twice.** The first run teaches you the timeline; the second teaches you the system.

## FAQ

**Is the war footing game demo free to play?**
Yes. The demo is a free download from the Steam store page, with no purchase required and no time limit mentioned in the listing.

**How long does the demo take to finish?**
The developers have not published an official completion time, and that is partly by design — scenarios are built to be replayed with different procurement and deployment choices, so your mileage depends on how deep you go.

**Can I run it on a basic laptop?**
Very likely. The minimum requirements are Windows 10 (64-bit), a dual-core 2.0 GHz processor, 4 GB of RAM, integrated graphics, and 500 MB of storage. That is about as light as modern strategy games get.

**Does demo progress carry over to the full game?**
The store listing does not advertise save transfers between the demo and the launch version, so plan on starting fresh. Treat your demo runs as practice, not as a save file you are protecting.

**Is the game about real countries?**
No. The developers describe it as a war between two fictional nations, with abstract violence that is not described in detail.
